# Research notes — Nasha Aur Masturbation Addiction Se Azaadi

Access date: 1 November 2026. These are synthesis notes, not copied book text.
Full citations and URLs live in `references.py` and the book's reference section.

## Scope and wording

- **R1–R3:** ICD-11 places compulsive sexual behaviour disorder (6C72) among impulse-control disorders. The clinically relevant pattern is persistent impaired control plus marked distress or impairment, not a universal frequency threshold. High sexual desire alone is not the disorder. Distress based entirely on moral disapproval is not sufficient for this diagnosis.
- **R2–R3:** Self-labelling as an “addict” is not a diagnosis. Medication effects, substances, mood episodes and other health conditions may need differential assessment. The book therefore uses “compulsive masturbation/porn pattern” in practical sections and reserves CSBD for the clinical framework.
- **R9:** Masturbation is a common sexual behaviour; frequency varies. Myths linking it to blindness, infertility or mental illness are unsupported. The book is explicitly shame-free and does not promote semen-retention claims.
- **R4–R8:** CSBD/PPU treatment research is developing. CBT-based psychotherapy has the most consistent emerging support; ACT, motivational work, mindfulness and relapse-prevention components may help. Many studies are small, male-dominated or methodologically limited. Do not promise a cure. Medication is specialist-led and may be off-label; it is never a self-medication instruction.

## Substance-use treatment and safety

- **R10–R12:** Substance-use disorders are treatable. Care should be individualised and may combine behavioural therapy, medication, social support and continuing review. Detox alone is not a complete long-term plan. Return to use is information for treatment adjustment, not proof of personal failure.
- **R16–R17:** Heavy prolonged alcohol use can produce dangerous withdrawal after abrupt cessation. Seizures, hallucinations, confusion and delirium require urgent medical care. The book does not provide an unsupervised alcohol taper.
- **R13:** CNS depressants such as benzodiazepines/sedatives can cause severe, potentially life-threatening withdrawal. They must not be stopped or tapered by a book-based schedule; medically supervised planning is required.
- **R14:** Opioid-use disorder has effective medications including buprenorphine, methadone and naltrexone, with selection and timing determined by qualified clinicians. Detox without continuing care is incomplete. Reduced tolerance after a period of non-use can increase overdose risk.
- **R15, R24:** Behavioural therapies are central for cannabis and stimulant-use disorders; contingency management has strong support for stimulants. No medication claims are generalised across substances.
- **R18–R19:** Tobacco cessation benefits from behavioural support and appropriate pharmacotherapy. India’s National Tobacco Quitline is 1800-11-2356.
- **R23:** Movement is framed as general health support, not a stand-alone addiction treatment. Start small and adapt to health/ability; do not prescribe a punishing exercise regime.

## Indian help resources

- **R19:** National Tobacco Quitline — **1800-11-2356**.
- **R20:** National Drug-de-Addiction Helpline — **14446**; NAPDDR includes identification, counselling, treatment, rehabilitation and reintegration.
- **R21:** Tele-MANAS — **14416 / 1800-891-4416**, 24/7 mental-health support and referral. KIRAN has been merged into Tele-MANAS, so the old KIRAN number is not presented as the primary resource.
- **R22:** Emergency Response Support System — **112** for urgent police/fire/medical emergencies.
- **R25:** India’s national survey supports the need for accessible evidence-based treatment, including outpatient services; it is not used for sensational prevalence claims.

## Editorial safeguards

1. Original Roman-script Hinglish throughout; no paragraphs copied from sources.
2. No diagnosis from a self-score, no guarantee, no “dopamine detox”, no semen-retention mythology, no punitive streaks.
3. No drug-use instructions, dosing schedules, DIY medication tapers or explicit sexual detail.
4. Clear escalation for seizures, hallucinations, severe confusion, breathing problems, unresponsiveness, injury, suicidal thoughts or danger to another person.
5. Consent, privacy and partner safety are non-negotiable. Blocking software is an optional barrier, never surveillance or partner-controlled punishment.
6. Composite examples are labelled fictional; no real patient story or testimonial is invented.
